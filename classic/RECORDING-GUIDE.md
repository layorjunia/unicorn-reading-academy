# Recording the Letter Sounds

The app needs one thing no computer can make: the 46 letter sounds, said
correctly, by a person. This is a ~20 minute session with the recording booth
(`Record Sounds.command`). Read this page once before you start — the whole
difference between audio that teaches reading and audio that teaches wrong
habits is in a few habits below.

## The one rule that matters most

**Never add "uh" to a stop sound.** /b/ is the tiny lip-pop at the start of
"bat" — not "buh". A child who learns "buh-a-tuh" cannot blend it into "bat".
Every sound below tells you whether to CLIP it short or HOLD it long.

## Before you start

- THE BUZZ TEST — learn this once before you record anything: put two fingers flat on the front of your throat and hum "mmm" — that vibration is your voice being ON. Now whisper "ssss" — no vibration, voice OFF. Every voice-ON sound must buzz under your fingers the whole time; every air-only sound must not buzz at all. Check with your fingers, not your ears.
- Two lengths, used everywhere: held sounds (m, n, s, f, v, z, l, r, sh, th, ng, and all the vowels) get about ONE second — count "one-mississippi" in your head. Quick sounds (b, d, g, p, t, k, j, ch, h, w, y) are over in under half a second.
- Record in a small, soft space — a closet full of clothes or a carpeted bedroom beats any room with bare walls — and turn off fans, AC, and anything that hums. Keep the mic a consistent 6-8 inches away and slightly OFF to the side of your mouth: stops like /p/, /b/, /t/ fire a puff of air that thumps the mic if you speak straight into it.
- Say the anchor word out loud first, then record just the sound: "cat... /k/". The word loads the correct sound into your mouth; recording it cold is when letter names sneak in. Leave a second of silence before and after every sound — trimming is easy; a clipped-off start is a redo.
- Do the whole set in one sitting with the same setup and distance. Keep it consistent the concrete way: before starting each batch, replay the very first clip you recorded in the sitting and match its loudness and your distance from the mic.
- Batch by type so you stay in one mode. Batch 1 — held sounds: m, n, s, f, v, z, l, r, sh, th, ng. Batch 2 — quick sounds: b, d, g, p, t, k, j, ch, h, w, y, kw, ks. Batch 3 — vowels: all the short and long vowels, oo, ow, oy. Batch 4 — glued chunks: ar, or, er, ing, ang, ank, ink, all, ant.
- Don't fight the volume of quiet sounds — /p/, /t/, /k/, /h/ are naturally near-silent, and pushing them louder is exactly what adds the "uh". But quiet doesn't mean inaudible: play each one back at the volume where your /m/ clip is clearly heard, and if the puff isn't plainly audible, re-record a little closer to the mic, still angled off to the side.
- Self-test before uploading — play these sequences back-to-back: b-a-t ("bat"), m-short-a-p ("map"), s-short-u-n ("sun"), r-short-e-d ("red"), and ch-short-i-p ("chip"). Each should sound like the word. Any sequence with an audible "uh" between sounds means re-record the offending clip.

## The most common mistakes

- The tacked-on "uh" (schwa) after stops — "buh, duh, guh, tuh, puh, kuh, juh, chuh". This is the number one blending-killer: buh-a-tuh can never blend into "bat". A tiny release is fine; a full vowel is not.
- Saying the letter NAME instead of the sound: "ay" for a, "are" for r, "ell" for l, "ex" for x, "double-u" for w, "why" for y. Letter names are a different thing the child learns separately.
- Whispering voiced stops: with the voice off, /b/ IS /p/, /d/ IS /t/, /g/ IS /k/. Voiced stops need the throat buzzing during the pop — check with two fingers flat on your throat (the buzz test), not your ears.
- Voicing unvoiced sounds: /p/, /t/, /k/, /s/, /f/, /sh/, /ch/ and the th in "thin" are pure air — no buzz under your fingers. Any throat buzz turns them into their voiced twins (b, d, g, z, v).
- Clipping continuants short. m, s, f, l, r, n, v, z, sh, th, ng should each be HELD for about one second — that hold is what makes continuous blending ("mmmaaap") possible.
- Splitting single sounds into pieces: "er" is not eh+r, "ng" is not n+g, "ar/or" are welded units, and glued chunks like -ing and -ank are said as natural wholes, not spelled out sound by sound.
- Vowels drifting mid-clip — starting on the right vowel and sagging into "uh" or growing a glide by the end. A vowel clip should sound identical at its start and its end.
- A hard g-pop after ng sounds: "ring" and every -ng glued chunk end on a nasal hum, never "ng-guh".

## The sounds

In the booth each sound shows this same coaching, with the app's own word
clips to listen to first. Say each sound **three times** with a pause between —
the import step picks the cleanest take.

### Consonant sounds

**/b/** — spellings: b — as in bat, big, bed
  
Press your lips together, turn your voice on, and pop them open — the tiny first sound of "bat" and "big". Stop the instant the pop happens.
  
*Watch out:* Saying "buh". The little puff after the pop is fine, but a full "uh" means the child hears buh-a-tuh instead of bat. And don't whisper it — fingers on your throat (buzz test): it must buzz during the pop, or /b/ becomes /p/.

**/k/** — spellings: c, k, ck — as in cat, kit, duck
  
Back of your tongue touches the roof of your mouth, then release a small puff of air with NO voice — the quiet start of "cat" and "kit". Say "cat" and stop right after the very first sound: a small puff off the back of your tongue, no buzz under your fingers.
  
*Watch out:* Saying "kuh". Fingers on your throat: a buzz during the pop itself means you've said /g/; a buzz that comes AFTER the puff is the tacked-on "uh" — cut the clip at the puff. Quiet, but not inaudible: at the volume where your /m/ clip is clear, this puff must still be plainly heard.

**/d/** — spellings: d — as in dog, dad, mud
  
Tap your tongue tip just behind your top teeth with your voice ON — the quick start of "dog" and "dad". Cut it off right after the tap.
  
*Watch out:* "Duh" is the classic. Tap and stop. And keep your voice on during the tap — fingers on your throat, it must buzz — or whispered /d/ turns into /t/.

**/f/** — spellings: f — as in fan, fish, fun
  
Rest your top teeth on your bottom lip and blow — hold "ffff" for a full second, the start of "fan" and "fish". Air only, no voice.
  
*Watch out:* Adding "fuh" at the end when you pull your lip away — let the air just fade to silence. And no buzz under your fingers, or it becomes /v/.

**/g/** — spellings: g — as in got, bag, pig
  
Back of the tongue up, voice ON, quick release — the start of "got" and the end of "bag". One short voiced pop.
  
*Watch out:* "Guh". Cut it off at the pop. Fingers on your throat: it must buzz during the pop — whispered /g/ is /k/.

**/h/** — spellings: h — as in hat, hen, hop
  
A quick, light puff of breath, like fogging a mirror — the start of "hat" and "hop". Quick, air only.
  
*Watch out:* Dragging it into a voiced grunt "huh". Keep it a quick whisper of air with no vowel attached — and check that at the volume where your /m/ clip is clear, the breath is still plainly audible.

**/j/** — spellings: j, ge, gi — as in jam, jet, jug
  
Tongue in /d/ position behind your top teeth, voice ON, and release into a soft buzzy "zh" — the start of "jam" and "jet". The soft buzzy tail IS part of the sound; keep it, then stop before any "uh" appears.
  
*Watch out:* "Juh" — holding past the buzz into a vowel. Self-test: play the clip back — if it sounds like plain /d/, you cut too early; if you hear "juh", you held too long. Keep the buzzy release, lose the vowel.

**/l/** — spellings: l, ll — as in lap, leg, shell
  
Tongue tip UP behind your top teeth and hum around it — hold "llll" for a full second, the start of "lap" and "leg".
  
*Watch out:* "Luh" comes from dropping the tongue tip at the end — keep it pressed up and just stop your voice. The letter name "ell" is the opposite error: it puts a vowel in FRONT — your tongue tip must already be touching and your voice already humming at the very first instant of the clip, nothing before the hum.

**/m/** — spellings: m — as in map, man, ham
  
Lips closed, hum "mmmm" like something smells delicious — hold it a full second. The start of "map" and "man".
  
*Watch out:* Opening your lips at the end, which adds "muh". Finish the clip with your lips still closed.

**/n/** — spellings: n, kn — as in net, nap, sun
  
Tongue tip behind your top teeth, hum through your nose — hold "nnnn" for a full second, the start of "net" and "nap".
  
*Watch out:* "Nuh", or the letter name "en" (which puts a vowel in FRONT). Just the held nasal hum, nothing before or after.

**/p/** — spellings: p — as in pan, pig, cup
  
Lips together, release a tiny puff of air with NO voice — the near-silent start of "pan" and end of "cup".
  
*Watch out:* "Puh" — a vowel escaping after the puff; stop the clip at the puff. Separately, if your throat buzzes during the pop (fingers on throat), you've recorded /b/. Angle off-mic so the puff doesn't thump — but quiet doesn't mean inaudible: at the volume where your /m/ clip is clear, the puff must still be plainly heard.

**/r/** — spellings: r, wr — as in run, red, rat
  
Say "red" and freeze on the very first instant — hold that for a full second. Tongue curled back and tense, not touching anything, lips slightly rounded — the growly start of "run" and "red".
  
*Watch out:* "Ruh" or the letter name "are". Self-test: play this clip, then your short-u and /n/ clips back-to-back — you should hear "run". If it plays back as "er-un" with a wobble in the middle, re-record with rounder lips and a tenser tongue.

**/s/** — spellings: s, ce, ci — as in sun, sit, bus
  
Hiss like a snake — "ssss" held for a full second, the start of "sun" and end of "bus". Air only.
  
*Watch out:* "Suh" at the release, or your voice sneaking in at the end — fingers on throat: no buzz allowed — which turns it into /z/. Pure quiet hiss, fading to nothing.

**/t/** — spellings: t — as in top, ten, cat
  
Tongue tip taps behind your top teeth with a tiny puff, NO voice — the quiet start of "top" and crisp end of "cat".
  
*Watch out:* "Tuh" — the most common mistake in the whole set. Fingers on your throat: a buzz during the tap means you've recorded /d/; a buzz after the puff is the tacked-on "uh" — cut the clip at the puff. And at the volume where your /m/ clip is clear, the tap must still be plainly audible.

**/v/** — spellings: v — as in vet, van
  
Top teeth on bottom lip like /f/, but turn your voice ON — hold "vvvv" like a little motor, the start of "vet" and "van".
  
*Watch out:* Letting the buzz drop out — fingers on throat: it must buzz the whole time, or it becomes /f/ — or releasing with "vuh". Keep the buzz steady, then just stop.

**/w/** — spellings: w, wh — as in wet, wig, when
  
Round your lips like blowing a kiss, then glide open quickly with voice on — the fast start of "wet" and "wig". Keep it short.
  
*Watch out:* The letter name "double-u", or a heavy "wuh". A tiny vowel tail is unavoidable on a glide — keep it as short and light as you can.

**/y/** — spellings: y — as in yes, yak
  
A quick springy glide, the very start of "yes" and "yak". Tongue high like "ee", then release fast. Short.
  
*Watch out:* The letter name "why", or a heavy "yuh". Quick and light — it should sound like you're about to say "yes" and stopped after the first instant.

**/z/** — spellings: z — as in zip, buzz
  
Buzz like a bee — "zzzz" held for a full second with your voice on, the start of "zip" and end of "buzz".
  
*Watch out:* The buzz going silent at the end — fingers on throat: it must buzz the whole time, or it decays into /s/ — or releasing with "zuh". Keep the voice humming, then stop clean.

**/sh/** — spellings: sh — as in ship, fish, shop
  
The quieting-a-baby sound — "shhhh" held for a full second, the start of "ship" and "shop". Air only, lips pushed forward.
  
*Watch out:* "Shuh" at the release. Hold the hush, then fade to silence — no vowel escape at the end.

**/ch/** — spellings: ch — as in chat, chip, much
  
Say the very start of "chat" or "chip" and stop instantly — one sharp burst with a short soft "sh"-like tail, no vowel.
  
*Watch out:* "Chuh", or softening it into "shh". Self-test: play your clip and imagine "ip" right after it — together they should make "chip", not "chuh-ip" or "choo-ip". A short sharp burst, not a held sound.

**/th/** — spellings: th — as in thin, bath, math
  
Tongue tip lightly between your teeth, blow air — hold a soft "thhh" for a full second, like in "thin", "bath", "math". NO voice, just quiet air.
  
*Watch out:* Voicing it into the th of "this/that" — fingers on throat: no buzz; the anchor words thin and bath use the quiet, air-only th. Also don't let it drift into /f/; the tongue must peek between the teeth.

**/ng/** — spellings: ng — as in ring, song
  
Say "ring" and freeze on the final hum — hold "ng" for a full second with the back of your tongue up and the sound coming through your nose, like the end of "song".
  
*Watch out:* Adding a hard g-pop at the end ("ng-guh") or saying it as two sounds "n" + "g". It's one held nasal hum with no release.

**/kw/** — spellings: qu — as in quick, quit
  
A quick /k/ puff that slides straight into /w/ — the start of "quick" and "quit". One smooth fast move, over in under half a second.
  
*Watch out:* "Kwuh" — landing on a vowel — or the letter name "cue". Self-test: play this clip, then your short-i and /k/ clips back-to-back. If you hear "quick", it's right; if you hear "kwuh-ick", cut the next take shorter.

**/ks/** — spellings: x — as in six, box, fox
  
A quick /k/ pop then a snake hiss — "ksss", the end of "six", "box", "fox". You can hold the hiss for a beat.
  
*Watch out:* The letter name "ex" — that puts an "eh" vowel in FRONT. Start straight on the k-pop with nothing before it, and keep the hiss voiceless — no buzz under your fingers.

### Vowel sounds

**/a/** — spellings: a — as in cat, hat, map
  
Say "cat" slowly and freeze the middle — "aaaa" with an open, wide jaw. Hold it steady for a full second, same sound as "hat" and "map".
  
*Watch out:* The letter name "ay", or letting the sound sag into "uh" as it fades. It must sound identical at the start and the end of the clip.

**/e/** — spellings: e — as in bed, pen, net
  
Freeze the middle of "bed" — a steady "ehhh", jaw half open. Same vowel as "pen" and "net". Hold one second.
  
*Watch out:* Drifting toward short-i (so "bed" would sound like "bid") or saying the letter name "ee". Check it against "bed" before recording.

**/i/** — spellings: i — as in sit, pig, win
  
Freeze the middle of "sit" — a quick, relaxed "ihhh", same as "pig" and "win". Hold it steady.
  
*Watch out:* The letter name "eye", or tensing into "ee" (so "sit" becomes "seat"). Keep the mouth relaxed, not smiling hard.

**/o/** — spellings: o — as in hot, dog, top
  
The doctor's "ahhh" — jaw wide open, the middle of "hot", "top", "mop". Hold it steady for a second.
  
*Watch out:* The letter name "oh" — completely different sound. Say "hot" first to load the right vowel, then hold just the middle — and check against "hot", not "dog" or "log", which in many American accents carry a different "aw" vowel.

**/u/** — spellings: u — as in sun, bug, cup
  
The plain "uhhh" in the middle of "sun", "bug", "cup". Relaxed mouth, hold it clean and steady for a second.
  
*Watch out:* The letter name "you", or grunting it too short. This is the one sound that really IS "uh" — give it a full, even beat like every other vowel.

**/ā/** — spellings: a_e, ai, ay — as in cake, rain, play
  
Say "ay" once at natural talking speed — the vowel in "cake", "rain", "play" — and don't stretch it. This one happens to match the letter name.
  
*Watch out:* Stretching it — stretching is exactly what creates the exaggerated "ay-EE" two-parter. Self-test: play the clip, then your /t/ clip — together they should sound like "ate".

**/ē/** — spellings: ee, ea, ey, y_e — as in see, read, key
  
Smile and hold "eeee" — the vowel in "see", "read", "key". Steady for a full second.
  
*Watch out:* Cutting it too short, or adding a little "yuh" tail at the end. Hold, then stop clean.

**/ī/** — spellings: i_e, igh, y_i, eye, ie — as in ride, light, sky
  
Say "eye" — the vowel in "ride", "light", "sky". It naturally glides from "ah" to "ee"; say it as one smooth word.
  
*Watch out:* Breaking it into two separate sounds "ah" + "ee", or chopping the glide off so only "ah" is left. One smooth motion, like saying the word "eye" itself.

**/ō/** — spellings: o_e, oa, ow_o — as in home, boat, snow
  
Round your lips and say a natural "oh" once at talking speed — the vowel in "home", "boat", "snow" — and don't stretch it.
  
*Watch out:* Stretching it into an over-rounded "oh-oo-w" tail — the stretch is what creates it. Self-test: play the clip, then your /t/ clip — together they should sound like "oat".

**/ū/** — spellings: u_e — as in cube, mule, cute
  
Say "you" — a tiny y-glide into "oo", the vowel in "cube", "mule", "cute" (kyoob, not koob).
  
*Watch out:* Dropping the y and recording plain "oo" (that's the moon sound, a different clip). Check with "cute" — if your recording blends into "coot", redo it.

**/oo/** — spellings: oo — as in moon, zoo, food
  
Tight round lips, ghost sound — "oooo" held steady, the vowel in "moon", "zoo", "food".
  
*Watch out:* Recording the OTHER oo — the short one in "book" or "look". Anchor to "moon" every time. Also don't let it drift toward "oh".

**/ar/** — spellings: ar — as in star, car, barn
  
The pirate sound — "arrr", one welded piece, the vowel-plus-growl in "star", "car", "barn". Open with "ah" and roll straight into the r growl, holding the growl.
  
*Watch out:* Splitting it into two sounds — flat cat-a then "rrr". It's one glued unit; say "car" and drop the c, don't build it from parts.

**/or/** — spellings: or — as in corn, fort, storm
  
One piece: "or" as in "corn", "fort", "storm". Round lips into "aw" flowing straight into the r growl — hold the growl for a full second.
  
*Watch out:* Adding "uh" after the r ("or-uh"), or saying two separate sounds. Say "corn" first, then record just the middle-to-end without the c and n.

**/er/** — spellings: er, ir, ur — as in her, bird, turn
  
One held growly vowel — "errr" as in "her", "bird", "turn". The whole sound is r-colored from the very start. Hold a full second.
  
*Watch out:* Saying "eh" then "rrr" as two pieces. There is no "eh" in it — your tongue is in r-position from the first instant. Say "her" and drop the h.

**/ow/** — spellings: ou, ow_ou — as in out, cow, loud
  
"Ow!" like you stubbed your toe — the vowel in "out", "cow", "loud". It glides ah-to-oo; say it as one smooth ouch.
  
*Watch out:* Confusing it with the ow in "snow" (that's long-o, a different clip) — anchor to "cow". And don't break the glide into "ah" + "oo".

**/oy/** — spellings: oi, oy — as in coin, boy, joy
  
"Oy!" like "ahoy" — the vowel in "coin", "boy", "joy". One smooth glide, aw-to-ee.
  
*Watch out:* Splitting it into "aw" + "yuh" or adding a "yuh" tail. One smooth piece, ending clean — say "boy" and drop the b.

### Glued sounds

**-ing** — spellings: ing — as in ring, king
  
Say "ring" and drop the r — "ing" as one natural chunk (same in "king"), ending on the held ng hum.
  
*Watch out:* Spelling it out as three crisp sounds "i-n-g", or adding a hard g-pop at the end ("ing-guh"). It ends on a hum, not a pop.

**-ang** — spellings: ang — as in sang, bang
  
Say "sang" and drop the s — "ang" as one natural chunk, same as in "bang". End on the ng hum.
  
*Watch out:* Building it from parts — flat cat-a + n + g. The vowel changes inside this chunk; trust how "sang" actually sounds and don't add a g-pop.

**-ank** — spellings: ank — as in thank, bank
  
Say "bank" and drop the b — "ank" as one chunk (same in "thank"), finishing with a light, quiet k.
  
*Watch out:* Forcing a flat cat-a and spelling out a-n-k. Say it the way it really sounds in "thank", and keep the final k an unvoiced puff — no "kuh".

**-ink** — spellings: ink — as in pink, wink
  
Say "pink" and drop the p — "ink" as one chunk (same in "wink"), ending with a light quiet k.
  
*Watch out:* Over-segmenting into i-n-k, or releasing the k with "kuh". One smooth chunk, crisp airy finish.

**-all** — spellings: all — as in ball, tall
  
Say "ball" and drop the b — "all" (aw + l) as one chunk, same as "tall". Finish holding the l with your tongue tip up.
  
*Watch out:* Using the flat cat-a ("al" as in "pal") — this chunk says "awl". And no "uh" after the l; end with the tongue still up.

**-ant** — spellings: ant — as in ant, plant
  
The word "ant" itself — said naturally as one chunk (same in "plant"), ending with a crisp, quiet t.
  
*Watch out:* Releasing the t as "tuh" ("an-tuh"). The final t is a whisper-quiet tap; stop right after it.

### Level 3 syllables (optional, later)

Level 3 teaches big words by chunks ("rab" + "bit"). Those chunks are normal
speech — no special technique — and the booth lists them after the core sounds.
Record them whenever; Level 3 sounding-out lights up as they land.
